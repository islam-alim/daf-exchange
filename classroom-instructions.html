Method 01 · Lesson planning

Plan a lesson learners can use.

Move from grammar goals to meaningful classroom action. This short tutorial uses a communicative task to make your next German lesson clearer, more active, and easier to assess.

35 minutes·For new & experienced teachers
Start the tutorial ↓
01

By the end, you will have

a communicative task and a compact lesson plan for a 60-minute A1–A2 class.

→

In this tutorial

1. Begin with action 2. Build the task 3. Shape the lesson 4. Reflect & adapt

Step 01

Start with what learners will do.

In a communicative approach, the destination is not “students understand the accusative.” It is a real-world action where they need language to achieve something. Grammar becomes a tool, not the finish line.

Example: food & shopping · A1

Instead of

“Practise accusative articles”

Try

“Buy ingredients for a shared dinner within a budget.”

✦
Your turn

Write one sentence: “At the end of this lesson, learners can…” Make it observable. Start with a verb such as choose, arrange, ask, compare, or explain.

Step 02

Give the action a reason.

A good task creates an information gap: each learner has something the others need. This makes interaction necessary instead of decorative.

  1. 01
    Set a situation

    “Four friends are planning a picnic for Saturday.”

  2. 02
    Split the information

    Give each pair a different supermarket flyer, dietary need, or budget.

  3. 03
    Name a result

    They agree on one shopping list and explain their choices.

Language support, not a script. Offer a small phrase bank: Wir brauchen… / Ich nehme… / Was kostet…? / Das ist zu teuer. Learners still decide what to say.

Step 03

Work backwards into a lesson arc.

Use the task as your centre. Everything before it prepares learners for success; everything after it helps them notice and improve.

0–8

Arrive

Show two picnic photos. Learners name what they recognise and vote: city park or lakeside?

8–20

Equip

Quickly sort food vocabulary and discover useful article patterns with real shopping cards.

20–43

Do the task

Pairs plan the picnic, negotiate a list, then meet another pair to compare plans.

43–55

Notice

Collect helpful learner sentences. Improve two or three together; keep the focus narrow.

55–60

Leave with a win

Exit ticket: “Heute kann ich…” plus one phrase the learner wants to reuse.

Step 04

Reflect like a thoughtful practitioner.

After class, collect evidence—not impressions. A 90-second note is enough to improve the next version.
